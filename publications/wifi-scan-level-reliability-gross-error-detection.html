An Intelligent Framework for Scan-Level Reliability, Accuracy Diagnostics, and Gross-Error Detection in Wi-Fi Fingerprinting-Based Indoor Positioning Systems

Ahmed Mansour, Wu Chen, Huan Luo, Mahmoud Adham, Jingxian Wang, Mahmoud Abdallah, and Duojie Weng

DOI: 10.1016/j.eswa.2026.134855

Read published articleSource code and Android implementationArchived software on ZenodoInteractive research explorerResearchGate record

Why this research matters

Average positioning accuracy cannot determine whether a particular Wi-Fi fix is trustworthy. This work examines scan-level evidence from the observed Wi-Fi scan, RSS-space similarity, radio-map coverage, candidate spatial geometry, and ambiguity to support reliability-aware positioning and future sensor-fusion design.

Abstract and scope

Wi-Fi fingerprinting supports indoor positioning, but individual estimates require scan-level reliability evidence to inform their acceptance, rejection, or weighting in sensor-fusion systems. This study develops 37 diagnostic quantities across five groups and retains 18 indicators for uniform evaluation across 10 building-level units and eight positioning estimators. It examines indicator–error associations, gross-error discrimination, confidently wrong failures, cross-dataset and cross-estimator robustness, and floor-aware behavior. A working Android implementation and corresponding Python analysis code demonstrate online diagnostic inspection.

Evaluation: 37 candidate quantities; 18 retained indicators; 10 building-level units; 54,520 training fingerprints; 13,629 query scans; 8 positioning estimators. Diagnostic assessment is not a calibrated probability of correctness.

Research questions answered

How can unreliable Wi-Fi fingerprinting estimates be diagnosed?

Inspect RSS compatibility, AP-set overlap, local radio-map support, candidate spatial spread, candidate-neighborhood instability and composite scan support scores rather than relying on the position coordinate alone.

Can the approach be integrated with other positioning methods?

The diagnostic layer operates alongside conventional estimators and informs future accept/reject/weighting policies for fusion pipelines; it does not train a new error predictor or demonstrate universally calibrated decision thresholds.

What is SQS?

SQS is a transparent composite of normalized scan and radio-map support components. It is not the probability that a given coordinate is correct.
